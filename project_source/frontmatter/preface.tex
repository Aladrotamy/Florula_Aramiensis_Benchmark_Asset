\chapter{Preface}

The Arami Uplands have no flora of their own. Collectors who worked in
the region over the past century sent their specimens to herbaria in
three different countries, and the names under which those specimens
were filed have since been revised many times. A forester, an
extension officer or a student who wants to identify a tree in the
Uplands today has to work from regional floras that treat the area as
a footnote.

This volume begins to close that gap. It treats the economic trees of
the Uplands: the timber species, the fruit and seed trees that feed
households and markets, and the trees whose bark, leaves or gums are
used in medicine and craft. These are the species that people in the
region most often need to name correctly, because a wrong name on a
seedling order, a timber permit or a conservation list has real
consequences.

The treatments are arranged by family, eight in this volume. Each
family opens with a short description and a key to its genera and
species. Each species treatment gives the accepted name and its
principal synonyms, local names, a description, notes on phenology and
habitat, the uses recorded in the literature, and the specimens on
which the treatment rests.

Later volumes will cover the shrubs, climbers and herbs of the
Uplands. Corrections and additional records are welcome and should be
sent to the Arami Uplands Herbarium.
