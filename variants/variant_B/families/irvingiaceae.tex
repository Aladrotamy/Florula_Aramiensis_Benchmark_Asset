\family{Irvingiaceae}{Bush mango family}
\label{fam:irvingiaceae}

Trees. Leaves simple, alternate and entire. Stipules large and
conical, enclosing the terminal bud and falling to leave a ring scar
round the twig. Flowers small, 5-merous, in panicles. Fruit a large,
fleshy or fibrous drupe. One species in the Arami Uplands.

\species{Irvingia gabonensis}{(Aubry-Lecomte ex O'Rorke) Baill.}
\label{sp:IRV01}

\field{Synonyms} \sciname{Mangifera gabonensis}{Aubry-Lecomte ex
	O'Rorke}

\field{Local and trade names} Yoruba: Oro; Igbo: Ogbono. Trade: Bush
mango.

\field{Status} Native.

\field{Description} Evergreen tree to 40~m with buttresses. Bark
grey-brown, smooth to finely scaly; slash yellowish. Leaves elliptic,
5--15~cm long, glossy above, with a short pointed tip. Terminal bud
enclosed in a conical stipule 1--4~cm long. Flowers small,
yellowish-green. Fruit an ellipsoid drupe 5--7~cm long, green turning
yellow, with a sweet, fibrous pulp around a single woody stone.

\field{Phenology} Flowering March to May; fruiting June to September.

\field{Habitat} Lowland semi-deciduous forest.

\field{Notes} \citet{harris1996} separated the bitter-fruited
\taxon{Irvingia wombolu} from this species. It has not
been recorded in the Arami Uplands.

\field{Uses} The kernels are dried, ground and used to thicken soups
(\textit{ogbono} soup), and the sweet pulp is eaten fresh. The tree is
increasingly planted in farms \citep{orwa2009}.
